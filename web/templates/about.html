How a card gets here

Six steps

  1. 01

    Seed

    Each (factor → outcome) pair starts as a one-off deep research run. We use Claude Sonnet for the most leveraged ones (~750 pairs, capped at $50 lifetime), and Codex/Claude collaboratively-produced JSON payloads for the rest. Every quantitative evidence row is required to cite a real PubMed ID.

  2. 02

    Verify

    The PMID validator hits PubMed esummary on every citation and rejects anything that doesn't resolve or whose title doesn't match. Single-letter "author" placeholders are rejected. n_participants is required for meta-analyses, RCTs, cohorts, case-control and cross-sectional rows.

  3. 03

    Tier

    Evidence rows are weighted by study type and quality. The summed score maps to a confidence tier:

    • A Strong — ≥2 high-quality meta-analyses or large RCTs, consistent direction, mechanism known. {{ tier_counts.get("A", 0) }} edges.
    • B Moderate — multiple cohorts + plausible mechanism, minor conflicts. {{ tier_counts.get("B", 0) }} edges.
    • C Emerging — early RCTs or strong observational, mechanism plausible. {{ tier_counts.get("C", 0) }} edges.
    • D Limited — mechanistic / animal / single study only. {{ tier_counts.get("D", 0) }} edges.
    • X Contested — evidence genuinely split. We show the disagreement on /myths and in the counter-evidence panel on each card. {{ tier_counts.get("X", 0) }} edges.
  4. 04

    Live update

    A launchd job fires every morning at 05:15. It pulls the last two days of new abstracts from PubMed and Europe PMC, asks local Gemma to extract any claims that match factors and outcomes already in the graph, re-scores affected edges, and queues anything that crossed into tier A or B for Claude review. The whole loop costs roughly $0 because Gemma runs on the user's Mac.

  5. 05

    Retraction watch

    A second weekly job (Sundays 04:30) re-checks every PubMed ID we cite. Anything PubMed has marked as retracted gets a red "RETRACTED" pill on its card and a reconsideration banner on the affected edge.

  6. 06

    Counter-evidence

    For tier-X and selected high-stakes edges, we show studies that disagree in a separate panel below the supporting evidence. The whole point of this site falls apart if you only read confirmation.

Limitations

What this won't do

  • Tell you what's right for you. We can show that "magnesium is associated with a small improvement in sleep onset latency in adults with low intake" — that's it. Whether you should take it is a clinician's call with your context.
  • Replace primary literature reading. Every card links its citations and you should follow them when the stakes are real.
  • Stay perfect. The graph drifts. We've built a retraction watcher and a daily re-score loop because we expect to be wrong about some things.

Everything else (daily ingest, dedup, prose rewrites) runs on local Gemma 4:26b via Ollama. Embeddings use nomic-embed-text via Ollama.

Not medical advice

This site summarises peer-reviewed research as objectively as we can manage. It cannot replace a clinician who knows your history, your meds, and your blood work.

Use it to ask better questions, not to treat yourself. If a card says a thing is harmful and you're already doing it, talk to a human professional before changing anything.
